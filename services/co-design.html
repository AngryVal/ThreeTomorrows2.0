Service 02 · Sprint

AI Co-Design Sprints.

Four to six-week sprints that find and ship your team's first useful AI workflows together. Built with the people who'll use them, not delivered to them.

Who this is for.

Teams with a clear problem and a willing group of people but no obvious starting point. Leaders who've watched AI training sessions land flat because nothing actually changed on Monday. Operations, marketing, customer service and finance teams sitting on repetitive work that AI could handle. Organisations that have tried an AI rollout, didn't see it stick, and want to do it better.

What you get.

Three outcomes from one sprint. One or two AI workflows shipped into production. A team that built them and therefore owns them. A playbook the rest of the business can use to repeat the pattern.

The co-design thesis is simple. AI adoption fails when it's delivered to people. It sticks when the people who'll use it build it. Three Tomorrows brings the AI expertise, the workflow architecture, and the technical chops. Your team brings the domain knowledge, the constraints, and the post-sprint ownership.

How it runs.

Week 1 · Discover

Map the work. Interview the team. Identify three to five workflow candidates worth the time.

Week 2 · Choose

Score the candidates. Pick one or two. Agree success criteria up front.

Weeks 3 and 4 · Build

Co-design workshops two to three times a week. By end of week 4 the workflows are running in beta with the sprint cohort.

Weeks 5 and 6 · Land

Refine based on real use. Document the playbook. Train the broader team. The handover happens through the people who built it.

Sample outcomes.

  • A marketing team running an agentic brief-and-draft workflow that cuts production time on routine work
  • A customer service team with an AI-assisted triage layer that lets humans focus on the calls that need humans
  • A finance team running an accounts-payable assistant that drafts entries for human review
  • A playbook the COO can hand to the next team to repeat the pattern

FAQ.

Four questions we get asked most
How is this different from a vendor implementation?

A vendor implements their product. A co-design sprint implements your team's understanding of their own work. The technology is in service of the team, not the other way around.

What technical stack do you build on?

Whatever fits. The defaults are the Claude ecosystem (Claude Cowork, Claude Code, MCP connectors) plus your existing tools (Google Workspace, Microsoft, Slack, Notion, CRM, data warehouse). No vendor lock-in.

How many people from our team need to be involved?

Three to six. A senior sponsor, two or three workflow owners, and the technical or operational lead who'll keep things running after the sprint ends.

What if the sprint doesn't land?

Success criteria are agreed in week 2. If the workflow doesn't meet them, we say so. The honest answer matters more than the win.
